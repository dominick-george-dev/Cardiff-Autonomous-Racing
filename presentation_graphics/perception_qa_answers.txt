PERCEPTION SECTION - Q&A ANSWERS (FS-AI 2026 Presentation)
=============================================================


Q1. Why not LiDAR?
-------------------------------------------------------------
We use stereo depth from the stock ZED2, camera-only. At the
ranges that matter for this task - cones are only useful to us
out to about 15 metres - stereo depth is accurate enough, and
skipping LiDAR halves our sensor integration overhead on a
vehicle we share with other subteams. It's not a dismissal of
LiDAR - it's the natural next upgrade if we need better range
or lighting robustness, but it wasn't the bottleneck this year.


Q2. Why EKF over particle filter / graph SLAM?
-------------------------------------------------------------
Our landmark count is small - around 70 cones on a full track -
and the noise on our detections is well-approximated as
Gaussian, so the classic EKF assumptions actually hold here.
EKF also gives us a per-timestep, directly inspectable state
vector, which matters a lot for debugging. Graph SLAM earns its
complexity at much larger map sizes than we ever operate at -
it would be solving a problem we don't have.


Q3. What happens on data association failures?
-------------------------------------------------------------
We gate association with a Mahalanobis distance test, so a
detection only matches an existing landmark if it's
statistically consistent with that landmark's current
uncertainty - otherwise it starts a new landmark instead of
corrupting an existing one. Wrong association is the classic
failure mode for EKF-SLAM, but we haven't observed it across
any of our validated laps, partly because cone colour acts as
a hard constraint on top of the distance gate - a blue
detection literally cannot match a yellow landmark.


Q4. What's your position error budget?
-------------------------------------------------------------
Track lanes are about 3 metres wide at their narrowest. Our
measured position RMSE is 0.67 metres with a mean heading error
around 1 degree, so we have comfortable margin to stay in-lane
even under our worst-case measured error, not just on average.


Q5. How do you know the sim transfers to the real car?
-------------------------------------------------------------
We're careful not to overclaim here. What we can say with
confidence is that the code path and message contracts are
identical between sim and real - every real-car difference is
a launch parameter, like the ZED2 topic names or the IMU
source, not a rewrite. What we can't yet claim is full sensor
realism - things like lighting conditions and real depth noise
characteristics are the known gap, and we say that openly
rather than hide it.


Q6. Is the IMU on the camera or the vehicle?
-------------------------------------------------------------
On the real car we use the vehicle's own IMU over
/ros_can/imu, not one built into the camera - yaw rate is
independent of mount position on a rigid body, so this is the
more robust choice. In simulation we use EUFS's IMU plugin
instead. Either way, the topic name is just a launch parameter,
so it's the same code underneath.


Q7. What about cone colour errors or unknown-class cones?
-------------------------------------------------------------
Our contract with Path Planning is: blue marks the left
boundary, yellow marks the right, and orange plus
unknown-class cones are explicitly excluded from boundary
logic - they don't get used to define the track edge. So a
misclassified orange or unknown cone can't accidentally redraw
a track boundary; it's a deliberate defensive choice in how the
map is consumed downstream, not just how it's built.
